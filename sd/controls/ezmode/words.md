What it is: tools for game modders. One ecosystem, four products: a crash reporter, community pages for gaming groups, mod hosting that pays creators, and a desktop mod manager. Each works alone and gets better connected.

For whom: players running heavy mod setups (Alex, Skyrim with ~150 mods, crashes entering Whiterun and has no idea why; wants to know: is it just me, is there a fix, can I show someone who can help). Mod authors (Sarah, who makes Combat Overhaul, gets "my game crashed idk" in Discord instead of real data, and is paid in fractions of a penny if at all). Community leaders running a modding group or guild who need a home that a platform can't take away. And their neighbours: the staff who moderate, and developers building on it.

A diagnosis made in Discord never reaches the next player who hits the crash. Creators aren't paid fairly. So ezmode's promises are the reverse of what they lived: your mod, your decision to delete it; creators earn 70%; only actually illegal content is removed; leaving is real, and that's what makes staying meaningful.

What it refuses: shaming creators with analytics, invented numbers, owning people's work.
